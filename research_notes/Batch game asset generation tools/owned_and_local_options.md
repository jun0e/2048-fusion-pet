## 1. Google AI Pro: which Google surfaces give clean (no visible watermark), commercially usable, batchable, reference-driven images, and at what cost?

### Takeaway
Two consumer surfaces stamp a visible watermark for an AI Pro user who lives in Vietnam (or India or South Korea): the **Gemini app** (only Ultra users get the toggle there) and **Flow** (the mark is applied automatically for residents of those countries). Neither offers an API.

Clean output comes from developer surfaces. Of these, **only the API (Gemini API, or Vertex AI, now renamed "Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform") is scriptable from the existing Node pipeline**. The API documents only invisible SynthID marking.

The $10/month AI Pro Developer Program credit is contested on AI Studio's prepaid billing. On the **postpaid Cloud billing account used by Vertex/Agent Platform** it is reported to apply automatically, according to Google Support. 117 images on Nano Banana 2.1 cost about $3.93 at list price, or about $1.97 with Batch.

### Cited Findings

**(a) Visible watermark, by surface and tier**
- Gemini app, current state: there is a "Media watermark" setting (Settings → Media Watermark). The article says: "If you are in India, South Korea, or Vietnam, you'll only see this setting if you have an AI Ultra subscription". The setting "only controls the visible watermark" and does not affect SynthID or Content Credentials (C2PA). It applies "only to Gemini Apps (web and mobile), not AI features in other Google products". - The toggle launched on 14 Aug 2026 and was rolling out over the following days. The default stays "On". It is excluded in "countries where it's required by law to keep them". It "also rolled out to Flow", and Search is next. — [9to5Google, 14 Aug 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/14/gemini-app-3-7-flash/)
- Older policy, now superseded by the toggle: at the Nano Banana Pro launch (20 Nov 2025), Google said it "will maintain a visible watermark (the Gemini sparkle) on images generated by free and Google AI Pro tier users" and "remove the visible watermark from images generated by Google AI Ultra subscribers and within the Google AI Studio developer tool". — [Google blog: Nano Banana Pro](https://blog.google/technology/ai/nano-banana-pro/)

- A conflicting report exists. A developer-forum thread is titled "Regression: forced visible STAR watermark breaks Gemini Nano Banana Pro image-to-image and Flow frame-to-video workflows". ## 4. Local/open-source on the user's Mac (M1, 16 GB, ~22 GiB free disk): which models and tools, which licences permit commercial use, and how fast, heavy and scriptable are they?

### Takeaway
The **commercially safe open models** are FLUX.2 [klein] **4B** (Apache-2.0), Z-Image-Turbo (Apache-2.0), Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (Apache-2.0) and Ming-Image-0.1 (MIT, RGBA output). The newest headline models are **non-commercial**: FLUX.1 [dev], FLUX.2 [klein] 9B and Qwen-Image 2.1 (RGBA, 10 references).

On a **base M1 with 16 GB**, a 1024 px image takes minutes, not seconds. The 20B Qwen edit model is effectively out of reach (RAM and disk), and LoRA training for FLUX-class models is impractical locally. A rented RTX 4090 trains a klein-4B LoRA in about an hour for about $0.50–$0.90.

ComfyUI (HTTP and websocket API), Draw Things (HTTP on port 7860 or gRPC) and mflux (CLI or Python) are all scriptable from Node.

### Cited Findings

- **Expected speed** on a base M1 is roughly **1.5–4 min per 1024² image** for klein 4B, Z-Image or FLUX schnell at 4–8 bits. - At those speeds, one pass of 117 images is about 3–8 hours. Generating 3–4 candidates per asset pushes that to a full day or more.
- **Transparency:** the commercially licensed native-RGBA options are Ming-Image (MIT, design-oriented) and LayerDiffuse (SDXL-family). Qwen-Image 2.1's RGBA output is research-only. The existing chroma-key step in the Node pipeline already solves transparency for any model. Avoid RMBG-2.0 for commercial use.

## 5. Key question: is a local pipeline realistic for ~117 consistent images, and what quality, consistency and time trade-offs does it bring compared with Gemini/OpenAI-class models?

### Takeaway
It is **technically possible but not attractive on this specific Mac.** The best commercially licensed open editors rank about 80–90 Elo below Nano Banana 2 and Pro in a public editing arena. On a base M1 with 16 GB, each 1024 px image takes minutes. Reaching style consistency realistically needs a trained LoRA, which in turn needs a rented GPU.

For about $4–8 of API usage, which the AI Pro $10 credit may cover, the Gemini API is far cheaper in time and better at multi-reference edits. A local or rented-GPU open pipeline makes sense only as a fallback, or for unlimited iteration once a style LoRA exists.

### Cited Findings
- Image-editing arena (Artificial Analysis, search excerpt; the snapshot date is uncertain and may predate Oct 2026):
  - GPT Image 1.5 (high) 1270, Nano Banana Pro 1250, Nano Banana 2 1243.
  - Best open-weights: HunyuanImage 3.0 Instruct 1223, **FLUX.2 [klein] 9B 1166** (non-commercial), **Qwen Image Edit Plus 2511 1162** (Apache-2.0).
  - Newer leaders such as MAI-Image 2.5/2.6 are reported elsewhere.
  - — [Artificial Analysis leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-image/arena/leaderboard-image); [OrcaRouter (secondary)](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/mai-image-2-5-pro-tops-image-editing-leaderboard)
- Reference capacity: Gemini Nano Banana 2.1 and 3.1 Flash Image accept up to 10 object reference images and Pro up to 6. — [Gemini API image docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation)
- Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 accepts multiple input images (two in the example). — [HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-Edit-2511)
- FLUX.2 klein supports multi-reference generation. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/black-forest-labs-launches-open-source-flux-2-klein-to-generate-ai-images-in)
- Time and cost data points:
  - Base M1 FLUX schnell about 335 s/image (8 GB, unquantized, 2024). — [mflux 0.4.0](https://pypi.org/project/mflux/0.4.0/)
  - klein 4B 30–40 s on an M1 Max; klein 9B 12–18 s on an RTX 4090. — [lilting.ch](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/flux2-klein-apple-silicon)
  - Rented 4090 $0.89/hr. — [RunPod](https://www.runpod.io/pricing)
  - klein-4B LoRA about 1 h, about $0.50. — [HF/BFL blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/black-forest-labs/flux-2-klein-lora)
- API pricing: 117 images × $0.0336 = $3.93 standard, $1.97 Batch (Nano Banana 2.1). — [Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)

### Inferences
- **Quality gap:** an 80-Elo gap means the frontier model wins about 61% of pairwise votes (Elo expected score 1/(1+10^(−80/400))). The gap is noticeable but not huge for single edits. It likely compounds in this project's chained edits (4 growth stages) and two-parent fusions with a style reference, where identity drift accumulates.
- **Consistency strategy differs:**
  - Gemini or OpenAI-class models get consistency from reference images plus prompts, which the current pipeline already does.
  - Open models usually need a **style or character LoRA** trained on 15–40 approved images (doable for about $1 on a rented 4090), plus seed and prompt discipline. They also need either an Apache-2.0 editor (Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, 20B and too heavy for this Mac) or klein 4B's multi-reference mode.
- **Time:**
  - Local base-M1 generation of 117 images with 3 candidates each takes roughly 9–24 h of machine time, an inference from 1.5–4 min per image.
  - A rented 4090 takes about an hour (about $1).
  - The Gemini API with the existing pipeline takes minutes (about $4–8).
- **Recommended stance for the report:**
  - Primary: Gemini API via Agent Platform on the $10/month credit, with Batch or Nano Banana 2.1 where available.
  - Free manual fallback: Figma Starter's 2-credit image model.
  - Open-source fallback: a **rented GPU running ComfyUI**, with FLUX.2 klein 4B or Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (Apache-2.0) plus a trained style LoRA, driven by the existing Node pipeline through ComfyUI's `/prompt` API.
  - Keep the M1 for previews and post-processing, not production generation.

### Gaps
- No side-by-side test of Nano Banana 2.1 against klein 4B or Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 on this sticker or soft-vinyl style was found. Quality conclusions rest on a general editing arena of uncertain date.
- No measured base-M1 timings were found for the recommended open models. Estimates are extrapolated from M1 Max and M1 Pro figures.
