# Draw stickers small, render toys large

The best fit for 2048 Siege is **one design per creature, drawn at three levels of detail.** The map tiles use a **thick-outline chibi sticker** (a dark outline plus a cream "sticker" rim, flat fills, at most one hard shadow tone). Roaming fusions use **the same sticker family, upgraded to full-body two-tone cel shading.** Namecard portraits use a **soft-vinyl "designer-toy" 3D render** painted onto its own coloured background plate. The sticker style wins both in-play layers because no other candidate scores well on all of these at once: readability at 55–60 pt under heavy effects, a number-first hierarchy, consistency from Gemini, clean background removal, cheap squash and tint animation in code, and small file size. The toy render wins the portrait because the portrait is the one layer that stands still, sits on its own painted plate (so it needs no background removal), and never shares space with a number. That is exactly where 3D's weaknesses disappear and its strengths count: it feels tactile and cute, it matches the cream clay UI, it looks different from Slime Legion's flat units, and Pokémon cards already treat 3D art as the "premium" tier. Four locked anchors keep the three layers recognisably the same creature: dominant colour, eye design, one signature element motif that only ever grows, and fixed "slots" for which parent supplies which part of a fusion. The emotional arc changes through staging, not through a new art style. Tiles stay cute and funny from 16 to 128. The biggest visual jump comes at the 256 fusion, which turns cool, angular and full-body. At 4096 the creature turns serene and majestic: a halo, symmetry, gold accents and slow floating motion. Two facts from the game code change the brief. First, the 4096 "ultimate" is any fusion plus a third "apex" element, so legendaries should be the 28 fusions plus 8 add-on halo/crown kits, not new creatures. Second, each fusion already has its own gameplay colour, and that colour should become the creature's main body colour. Fix the generation pipeline before making a single asset: remove the competitor names from the style prompt, enable billing, put Nano Banana 2.1 first, stop the silent fallback to older models, add reference-image support, and move glows into code. All scores and numeric targets below are synthesis, not measurement: no source compares these art styles head-to-head.

## Stickers win both play layers; a vinyl toy wins the namecard

Each layer was scored 1–5 on the eleven criteria in the brief. The weights reflect what each layer has to do. On tiles, readability, hierarchy and element identity count triple. On roaming sprites, readability and code animation count triple. On the portrait, emotional pull, fit with the UI, market differentiation and production count triple. Throughout this report, a sentence with a link is sourced; scores, weights, numeric targets and the brief itself are my inference unless a link is attached. The column keys are the same in all three tables: **R** readability at small size under VFX, **H** information hierarchy (number, border or skill state stays on top), **P** production scalability, Gemini consistency and how well it keys out, **F** whether an evolution family reads as one family, **E** element identity beyond colour, **A** cheap code animation, **C** "care for pets" and the cute-to-majestic arc, **U** fit with the cream clay UI and the chibi enemies, **W** web performance and file size, **M** market fit and differentiation, **I** IP safety. Weighted totals are normalised to 100.

### Map tiles: the number owns the square, so the creature must be a sticker

The closest precedent is Threes!. Its team tried close to two dozen picture themes (sushi, chess pieces, animals, military insignia), found testers confused, and "ended up where they started, with numbers," keeping small faces that "grow in character with their size" ([Wikipedia: Threes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threes)). Illustrator Greg Wohlwend showed growth by adding one feature at a time ("an eyeball, a mouth, then two eyes"), so the tiles looked "cut from the same cloth as opposed to separate species" ([Sirvo Threemails](http://asherv.com/threes/threemails/)). The space is tight. The original 2048's mobile tile is **58 × 58 px with a 35 px two-digit number, about 60% of the tile's height** ([2048 main.css](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gabrielecirulli/2048/master/style/main.css)), so a creature in a 55–60 pt tile can only use the room the number gives up. Accessibility rules tighten this further. Xbox's contrast guideline asks for **4.5:1 for standard-size text, measured against the lowest-contrast part of a non-solid background** ([XAG 102](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/102)). A 20–28 pt numeral is standard-size on a phone, so either the art under it must have a narrow range of light and dark, or the number needs its own double outline. Flat, outlined art makes both easy.

The sticker style meets these constraints by its nature. Google's own prompt guide offers a sticker template built on "bold, clean outlines, simple cel-shading" and says that specific visual detail gives consistent results ([Gemini image generation docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation)). The one detailed Nano Banana Pro sprite case study kept a consistent cartoon style across about 74 sprites using "bold black outlines 2-3 pixels wide, flat colors." The same study found that background removal worked only with exact #00FF00 green, a **2–3 px white outline as a buffer**, and HSV-based keying, while dark outlines on dark or magenta backgrounds were "catastrophic" ([roboticape.com](https://roboticape.com/2026/03/07/generating-game-sprites-with-gemini-image-generation-nano-banana-pro-lessons-learned/)). In PixiJS, `tint` multiplies colours ([html5gamedevs](https://html5gamedevs.com/topic/11122-sprite-whitening)), so a flat-filled sprite takes element and status tints while its dark outline stays dark. A local synthetic benchmark in the notes (16 sprites in a 512² atlas) compressed flat outlined art to **28.9 KB in lossless WebP, against 127 KB for textured clay and 174 KB for painterly**. Treat those as rough ratios, since the art was procedural. Guidelines insist that no essential information rely on colour alone ([Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-no-essential-information-is-conveyed-by-a-fixed-colour-alone/)). A thick-outlined silhouette motif plus a glyph badge carries element identity at 25 pt; soft gradients do not.

The alternatives each fail somewhere specific. **Flat vector without an outline is a real runner-up on tiles.** The tile background is a controlled plate, and Threes proves the look works. But it reads as an icon rather than a pet, and it loses its edge as soon as the same creature leaves the tile. Soft 3D and clay renders turn into a mid-value blob at 55 pt. Their baked highlights fall in the same near-white band as numbers and explosion cores, and their soft contact shadows key badly. Pixel art fails on both generation and animation. A GPT Image 2 test asked for 12 colours and got **44,725**, with only 2% of colour changes on the intended grid ([rangy.ai](https://rangy.ai/blog/ai-pixel-art)). Non-integer scaling and rotation cause shimmer and jagged edges ([Excalibur.js docs](https://excaliburjs.com/docs/pixel-art)), and squash/stretch is exactly that kind of transform. Reviewers also benchmark pixel creature games directly against GBA-era Pokémon ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/reviews/coromon-review/)).

### Roaming fusions: keep the sticker family, let silhouette and motion carry identity

At 256 the job changes. The creature no longer frames a number. It has to survive among ~30 pt enemies, projectiles and 8-frame explosions. Riot's clarity rules say the most important thing on screen should draw the most attention, and that visual impact should match gameplay impact ([Riot: Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)). A Riot character artist argues that detail spread evenly over a character does about as much as no detail at all ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/character-design-shape-language-and-readability/)). The sticker style therefore upgrades rather than switches. Fusions are full-body in a 3/4 view, with two hard shade tones plus a rim light in the element colour, the same outline weight on screen as the tiles, and a more angular silhouette. Keeping creatures in the middle of the brightness range leaves the near-white band free for effect cores and hit flashes.

The notes disagree on whether roaming pets should go 3D to stand apart from the enemies. Slime Legion is a merge plus roguelike tower defense in which monsters defend a forest against invading "brutal heroes," and its units carry elements too ([App Store: Slime Legion](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/slime-legion/id1664686966)). If the pets share its flat chibi look, 2048 Siege risks reading as "Slime Legion with a 2048 board." The tile-icon notes answer that mixing pre-rendered 3D sprites with flat sticker tiles causes a style clash. I side with the sticker style for roaming, for three reasons. Baked 3D lighting rotates with the sprite. Squash smears baked highlights. And 28 soft-edged renders multiply lighting drift and keying trouble. Separate pets from enemies with cues the enemies never get instead: a cream sticker rim, a warm element-coloured rim light and an element ground ring for pets; a dark, desaturated outline and a red-tinted shadow for heroes. Xbox's guideline cites For Honor's black-then-white double outline as a way to read on both dark and light backgrounds ([XAG 102](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/102)). The toon-outlined 3D render is the runner-up if the team later wants roaming sprites closer to the portraits.

Size also conflicts between the notes: "~40–60 px sprites" in the tile notes against "1.3–1.6× tile size" in the growth-arc notes. Enemies are ~30 pt and lanes are roughly one tile wide. A fusion at 1.6× tile size (~95 pt) would span one and a half lanes and hide the enemies it fights, which breaks Riot's hierarchy. **I recommend fusions at about 1.0–1.2× tile size (56–70 pt, roughly twice an enemy's height) and legendaries at 1.3–1.5× including the halo.** Express extra mass through slower motion and a dust puff on landing. The size arc still works: a baby's visible head on a tile is ~25 pt, and the fusion's full body at ~65 pt is about 2.5× that.

### Namecard portrait: a soft-vinyl render on its own painted plate

Shipped games already pair simple in-game art with rich portrait art. Supercell's Creature Shop makes hi-res renders for Clash Royale's in-game UI and marketing alongside the in-game assets. It exports **"_low" and "_high" versions of the same base design**, makes materials rougher than real life so colour gradients read clearly, and steers value contrast toward faces ([Adobe Substance 3D Magazine](https://www.adobe.com/products/substance3d/magazine/supercell-helsinki-creating-stylized-content-for-clash-of-clans-and-clash-royale.html)). Pokémon TCG gives the 3D-CG studio PLANETA many of its premium GX, V and ex cards ([pkmncollectors: PLANETA Igarashi](https://www.pkmncollectors.com/artists/planeta-igarashi); [Bulbapedia: PLANETA](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/PLANETA)). Both precedents match the brief's wish to make the 3D portrait a reward that appears at fusion.

On the namecard, 3D's usual weaknesses disappear. The portrait does not squash or rotate. It shows no number. And because it sits on its own element-coloured plate painted into the image, it needs no background removal at all. Turning a 2D character into a collectible-figure render is also Gemini's best-exercised use case: the 2025 figurine trend produced **200M+ images in two weeks**. Practitioners recommend naming materials and adding "the face is exactly the same" to keep identity ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/338994/how-use-google-nano-banana-ai-transform-selfies-digital-figurines?amp=1)). The market supports the look. Stylized art dominated the top 100 new mobile games of 2024, **75 stylized against 9 realistic** ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/analyzing-trends-in-the-top-100-new-mobile-games-in-2024-cm4i3q2zd000f10m89woph23e)). Full-3D ad creatives have been a proven casual direction since 2024–25 ([Appodeal](https://appodeal.com/blog/the-ads-everyone-will-be-watching-in-2026/)). Merge Mansion builds items in Blender and ships them as 2D, so the look needs no 3D runtime ([Metacore](https://metacoregames.com/news/in-a-nutshell-how-merge-mansions-game-art-evolves-from-first-sketches-to-final-assets/)). The vinyl look also matches the game's existing HUD tokens, `--cream: #fffaf2` and soft "clay" drop shadows ([src/ui/hud.css](file:///Users/lap14657/merge-ball-td-siege/src/ui/hud.css)).

The layout code adds a constraint the notes did not consider. `stage.js` makes the world 9.5 units wide and fits it to the available height ([src/render/stage.js](file:///Users/lap14657/merge-ball-td-siege/src/render/stage.js)). On a 375–393 pt phone with ~56–60 pt cells, the strip between the board edge and the screen edge is only about 65–85 pt (my estimate; it depends on the HUD bar heights). **A side namecard therefore rests at roughly 56–64 pt, about the size of a tile,** and only grows to ~120–160 pt during the activation cut-in. That rules out detail-heavy variants. Choose matte vinyl with large soft gradients, a strong rim light in the element colour to separate the silhouette, the face as the highest-contrast zone, and a bust crop so the face is 3–5× larger than on the roaming sprite. Clay is charming but struggles to look majestic at Legendary, and its fingerprint texture costs bytes. A glossy PVC figurine is the most Gemini-proven look, but its speculars fight the matte UI and the model tends to add bases and packaging. Painterly 3D is noisy at rest size. A hi-res sticker portrait would score 74, so the brief's 3D requirement costs nothing on this scorecard: it buys differentiation and emotional pull.

## Four anchors make three renderings read as one creature

Supercell's low/high model is the rule to copy: one canonical design per creature, exported three ways, never re-imagined. In practice, generate a hi-res canonical sticker image for each creature first. The **tile art is a crop and downscale of the canonical baby**, the **roaming sprite is the canonical fusion's full body**, and the **portrait is rendered from the canonical sticker passed in as a character reference**. Pokémon designers work final form first: Haxorus's fangs guided its whole line, with the most dramatic change saved for the last stage ([Nintendo Dream 2011, trans.](https://lavacutcontent.com/ken-sugimori-nintendo-dream-6/)). The same order applies here on paper. Write the 28 fusion and 8 apex-kit specs first, because the 8 babies must carry exactly the motifs the fusions will recombine. Then generate babies first, because every later image uses them as references.

The four anchors are colour, eyes, motif and slots. **Colour:** Pokémon keeps a line's dominant colour class in **25 of 27 starter lines** ([PokeAPI data](https://pokeapi.co/docs/v2)), so babies hold their element colour from 16 to 128. Fusions are a special case. The game code already gives every fusion its own colour (Frostnova is cobalt `#5b8cff`, Plasma hot pink `#ff4fb8`, Railgun gold `#ffd700`) ([src/sim/classes.js](file:///Users/lap14657/merge-ball-td-siege/src/sim/classes.js)). Make that the fusion's dominant body colour, so the tile plate, projectiles, roaming sprite and namecard all agree, and carry the two parents through shape and 30/10 accent colours. **Eyes:** keep the shape family and highlight style even as eyes mature. **Motif:** the signature element motif transforms rather than vanishing, the way Froakie's bubble collar became Greninja's tongue scarf ([DBpedia: Greninja](https://dbpedia.org/page/Greninja)). **Slots:** fusions follow a fixed split. Onsager's fusion generator took "the body and overall shape" from the first Pokémon and "the face and color scheme" from the second ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/article/57026-pokemon-fusion-website-twitter)). Cassette Beasts swaps animated parts procedurally, and its co-founder admits many of its 14,000 fusions "will look similar" ([Film Stories](https://filmstories.co.uk/gaming/creature-collecting-gets-an-analogue-upgrade-in-cassette-beasts/)). With only 28 fusions, hand-specifying each one while keeping the slot discipline gets the best of both. 2048 Siege's own projectile rule is already "the first element's shape in the second element's colour" ([README.md](file:///Users/lap14657/merge-ball-td-siege/README.md)), so the creature slot rule is: **body and silhouette from parent A, head crest or tail motif from parent B, fusion colour dominant.** Fix a canonical A/B order for each pair (for example from the name: Blue Flame or Frostnova is a fire body in ice colours). Finally, keep one baby "vestige" visible in every adult form, as Serperior keeps the folded hands Snivy was given to look less frightening ([Nintendo Dream 2011, trans.](https://lavacutcontent.com/ken-sugimori-nintendo-dream-3/)).

**Legendaries are fusions with an apex kit, not new creatures.** The code defines the 4096 ultimate as a fusion plus a third, apex element whose signature names it: Sun, Timestop, Tempest, Plague, Meteor, Vortex, Midas or Lifebloom ([src/sim/classes.js](file:///Users/lap14657/merge-ball-td-siege/src/sim/classes.js)). Any of the 28 fusions can take any of its six missing elements, so unique art per combination is out of the question. The scalable answer is 8 ascension kits (halo, crown or ring sprites, one per apex element) composited in code onto the fusion sprite, plus an optional "ascended" edit of each fusion portrait. Dragonite shows which continuity rules to keep. It went from a blue serpent to an orange winged biped that is shorter than its previous stage, and critics have called it a "downgrade" for fifteen years ([Wikipedia: Dragonite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragonite)). So never change a line's dominant colour, never shrink a stage, and never make a later stage rounder or more babyish.

The "care" goal favours pushing baby features hard. Baby-schema faces (round face, high forehead, big eyes) raise both rated cuteness and the stated wish to care for the creature ([Glocker et al. 2009](https://repository.upenn.edu/cog_neuro_pubs/11)). The effect extends to animal faces ([Borgi et al. 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00411/pdf)). In one study, children who cared for a virtual dog regarded it "as a real pet with its own emotions" ([SFU](https://sfu.ca/education/research/research-projects/computer-simulated-animals-empathy.html)). These came from search summaries, so treat them as directional. Game Freak's guidance matches the arc: starters "shouldn't look too strong from the very beginning — once they evolve, that's when they start to look powerful" ([Nintendo Dream 2011, trans.](https://lavacutcontent.com/ken-sugimori-nintendo-dream-3/)). Ken Sugimori also deliberately adds "something uncool" to keep cool designs memorable ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/pokmon-designer-on-balancing-cool-or-cute-pokmon-by-adding-uncool-or-uncute-features/)). Three in-play bridges tie the layers together. At 128→256 the creature visibly hops out of its tile. The tile it leaves becomes a split-colour "den" that keeps the full-size number. And when a skill fires, a ribbon runs from the namecard to the roaming sprite.

## The style brief

### Tile layout: number up top, creature peeking over the sill

Build the 56–60 pt tile in this order, back to front. First the **tile plate**: the existing bevelled clay block in a tinted or shaded version of the element colour. Next the **creature**: a head-and-shoulders crop anchored to the bottom inner edge, filling the lower ~45% "as if peeking over a windowsill." On top of that sits the **number**, centred in the upper ~55%, rendered as live BitmapText rather than baked into the sprite, as both XAG 102 and the PixiJS performance guide recommend ([PixiJS 8 performance tips](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/concepts/performance-tips)). Then the **4-piece progress border**: a dark outer line plus a light inner line, at least 3:1 against both the art and the board. Last, a **monochrome element glyph badge** (white glyph, dark outline) clipped over the bottom-right corner like a collar tag. The number may overlap the top of the creature's head but never its eyes, and any crest or ears behind the number stay mid-value. The element colours already in the code (fire `#ff7a3d`, ice `#7fd4ff`, lightning `#ffe14d`, poison `#b46cff`, earth `#c79a62`, wind `#bff7e6`, metal `#c9d3df`, nature `#4cd964`) span only **L\* 60–93**. By my calculation, a cream numeral fill reaches only **1.1–3.1:1** against these plates and fails the 4.5:1 target, while a dark plum numeral (`#2B1D2E`) reaches **4.95–13.4:1** on every plate. So the number should be **dark-filled with a 1.5–2 pt cream outer stroke**: the double-outline principle, which reads on the plate and over the creature's dark outline alike. The same calculation shows two pairs nearly tied in greyscale, **fire and earth (L\* 66 vs 67)** and **ice and metal (81 vs 84)**. Separate them by value as well as hue. A workable value plan for creature bodies and plates keeps wind, lightning and ice light, fire mid, and darkens nature, metal, earth and poison into mid-dark steps, without touching the gameplay colours used by the UI.

### Shape signatures per element

Lab work finds people prefer curved contours and respond to sharp angles as mild threats ([Bar & Neta 2007](https://canlab.unl.edu/sites/unl.edu.cas.psychology.cognitive-and-affective-neuroscience/files/media/file/BarNetaNeuropsych2007.pdf)). Downward-pointing V shapes are read as "unpleasant" faster than circles ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/05/120529074617.htm)). So every pet body is circle-based, which is safe and lovable, and element identity lives in a motif attached to that body. Candy Crush shows the principle at scale by pairing each candy colour with a distinct shape ([Candy Crush Wiki](https://candycrush.fandom.com/wiki/Candy)). A shared "bean" body plan for all eight babies also serves IP safety: babies differ by motif, not by animal archetype.

Fusions show two signatures at once, one per slot. The 28 fusions are then learnable from 8 shapes rather than 28 hues, and element identity survives for colourblind players and under temporary status tints. Xbox's guideline asks for at least two channels for critical information ([XAG 103](https://devdocs.xbox.com/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/103)); here the channels are shape, glyph and colour. Offer optional colour remapping rather than a full-screen filter, which colourblind players dislike ([Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-no-essential-information-is-conveyed-by-a-fixed-colour-alone/)).

### Palette and outline rules

Hold each creature to four or five colours plus outline: an element base, one hard shade, one accent, a belly or face mask, and eye whites. Each stage escalates by adding motif pieces, never new hues, until the legendary adds a single gold or white prestige accent. Fusions use roughly 60% fusion colour, 30% motif-parent colour and 10% accent. Use one outline colour everywhere, a warm **dark plum (`#2B1D2E`)** that sits better beside the cream UI than pure black, plus a **cream (`#FFFAF2`) outer sticker rim** on tiles and roaming sprites only. Outline weight must be normalised at final display size, not at generation size: about **1.5–2 pt dark line and 1–1.5 pt cream rim on screen** in both layers (roughly 4.5–6 px and 3–4.5 px at 3× pixel density). The cheapest way to guarantee this is to add the cream rim in post-processing by expanding the alpha mask at the final resolution, while still asking for the rim in the prompt because it also buffers the background key. Keep creature colours mid-value and moderately saturated, and reserve near-white for numbers, effect cores and hit flashes. Portraits drop the ink outline. Their silhouette comes from the element rim light, and the frame sprite supplies a cream stroke. Enemies keep their own dark outline with no cream rim and a red-tinted shadow, so friend and foe differ in shape and value, not just hue. Generate creatures that contain green (nature, Swamp, Toxic Shock and the like) on magenta, and all others on green, as `assets.config.mjs` already does per asset.

### Motion by stage

Animation timing signals personality and mass: snappy anticipation reads as lively, while slow, drawn-out motion reads as heavy or confident ([Pixune: Anticipation](https://pixune.com/blog/anticipation/)). Squash and stretch is one of the twelve classic principles ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_and_stretch)). The arc therefore runs from rubbery babies to rigid, floating legendaries.

Lightning and wind fusions can stay fast, but should switch from bouncy-fast to precise-fast: clean dashes with no squash. Expression range narrows as stages rise. Babies get sweat drops and tears, fusions a smirk, legendaries one serene face. The narrowing itself reads as growing up. Two implementation rules keep this cheap in PixiJS. Do white hit-flashes with a pre-built white silhouette sprite, never per-sprite filters, because filters are expensive. And keep additive-blend effects in their own layer, because switching blend modes breaks batching ([PixiJS 8 performance tips](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/concepts/performance-tips)). Cuteness also has a cost when a pet dies. Tamagotchi "death" caused real distress and resets ([Wikipedia: Tamagotchi effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamagotchi_effect)), and Fire Emblem's own leads conceded that permadeath kept people from trying Awakening ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-04-03-intelligent-systems-divided-over-fire-emblem-awakenings-casual.html)). Give babies a "scared" eye state before a block is lost, and make death a gentle poof into element sparkles rather than anything harsh.

### Portrait framing and skill-button states

Frame the portrait as a rounded-rect bust crop in 3/4 view, facing the playfield, with the face covering about 70% of the frame's width. Paint the element-coloured radial plate into the image itself, and lay a frame sprite over the square texture so no runtime mask is needed. Below or overlapping the bottom edge sits a cream clay name pill. Fusion frames split into the fusion colour and the motif parent's colour, with both glyphs. The legendary frame turns gold or animated foil and adds the apex emblem. The fusion names in code are phenomena ("Plasma," "Railgun," "Corrosion"), which read as skills rather than pets. To serve the care goal, give each creature a pet name and show the phenomenon as its skill on the pill. The skill-button behaviour follows converging mobile conventions. AFK Journey's energy bar "will shine" when an ultimate is ready ([BlueStacks guide](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/afk-2-journey/afkj-beginners-guide-en.html)). Pokémon UNITE's signature move needs a full meter ([WikiDex](https://www.wikidex.net/wiki/Movimiento_Unite)). Clash Royale's August 2026 change made champion abilities single-use per deployment, turning them "from a rate resource into a timing resource" ([Trophy Coach](https://trophycoach.com/guides/single-use-abilities-explained)).

### Gemini prompt and reference strategy

Use **Nano Banana 2.1 (`gemini-nano-banana-2.1`) as the default model.** Google calls it "the primary high-efficiency workhorse" and recommends it for new projects ([Gemini image generation docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation)). It costs **$0.0336 per 1K image, or $0.0168 through the Batch API** ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Reserve Nano Banana Pro ($0.134 per image) for hard fusions. At ~100 assets × ~4 attempts, the notes estimate about **$13 standard or $7 batch on 2.1, against about $54 or $27 on Pro**, so cost is not the constraint; quota and drift are. Google documents no seed parameter, so keep every raw image and every approved reference so assets can be regenerated on a successor model. The 2026 preview model IDs were shut down within about seven months ([Gemini deprecations](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deprecations)). Generate one character per call, because "style consistency drops with complexity," and expect proportions to drift between calls ([roboticape.com](https://roboticape.com/2026/03/07/generating-game-sprites-with-gemini-image-generation-nano-banana-pro-lessons-learned/)). After generation, normalise each sprite by a head-height target, then snap the sticker layers (not portraits) to one master palette. Because the notes conflict on which model has dedicated style-reference slots, **send at most five images per call, and pass the "golden" approved images with explicit text such as "match the rendering of images 3–4."** That works under either reading of the docs, stays inside Pro's 5-character and Nano Banana 2.1's 4-character limits, and matches advice that fidelity holds best with six or fewer references ([laozhang.ai](https://blog.laozhang.ai/en/posts/nano-banana-pro-face-consistency-guide)).

The generation order follows the anchors. First, generate the 8 babies at stage 16 with text only until two of them are approved as golden references. Second, derive stages 32, 64 and 128 as **targeted edits** of the approved previous stage, which the docs support as "change only…" instructions. This is the Threes-style "add one feature" approach. Third, generate each fusion with both parents as character references and two golden fusions as style references. Fourth, generate each portrait from the approved hi-res fusion sticker plus two golden portraits, using a lighting rig and camera written once and pasted verbatim, since lighting drift across 28 creatures is the biggest untested risk. Fifth, generate the apex kits as flat sticker objects with no glow; the glow is added in code. Sixth, and optionally, make ascended portraits as edits of the approved fusion portraits. Every prompt carries a per-creature identity spec: element(s), body slot, motif slot, three locked hex colours, eye spec, vestige, and features to avoid. Draft skeletons follow (untested; adapt per creature):

## Guardrails: Pokémon distance and a pipeline that can hold a style

### IP safety comes from shapes, prompts and paperwork, not from the art style

The legal danger is resemblance, not mechanics. A Shenzhen court awarded The Pokémon Company **107M yuan (about $15M)** against the makers of "Pocket Monster Reissue" ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/the-pokemon-company-wins-15-million-copyright-infringement-lawsuit-against-china-based-game-developers/)). Defendants later settled and apologised for having "extensively used design elements" of the series ([WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/legal/item-47199)). Palworld was judged in public by "nearly identical proportions" and near-identical faces ([Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/did-palworld-really-plagiarize-pokemon/)). The actual lawsuit was over patents for throw-to-capture and gliding, and it has since been narrowed, with damages cut to ¥5M ([Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/almost-two-years-ago-nintendo-sued-the-creators-of-palworld-what-the-hell-was-going-on,1582112/)). 2048 Siege spawns pets from merges, so its mechanics sit far from those patents. Its overall impression does not: eight elements that map almost one-to-one onto Pokémon types, cute evolving creatures, fusions and "legendaries." Generation itself raises the risk. In one study, image models reproduced copyrighted characters about **48% of the time from 60-word descriptions that avoided names**, and prompt rewriting plus negative prompts **cut detections by 86%** ([He et al., arXiv 2406.14526](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.14526v2)). The guardrails follow from that. Strip franchise names from every prompt. Never combine signature keyword sets (the "IP traps" in Table 5). Give all babies one shared bean body plan, and draw fusions from materials and folklore rather than real animals. Run a 55 pt black-silhouette test asking testers "which existing character is this?". Reverse-image-search every approved sprite. Keep a design log of references for each creature. Give the 8 base pets a human art pass, since all 28 fusions inherit from them. Avoid red-and-white sphere motifs and "starter trio" marketing, and get a legal review before soft launch. By style, pixel art raises the GBA-Pokémon association, a 3D portrait is risky only if its shapes match, and the sticker style's main risk is looking like Slime Legion's units. That last point matters because the README's enemy roster is titled "Enemies (from Slime Legion footage)" ([README.md](file:///Users/lap14657/merge-ball-td-siege/README.md)), so the hero designs deserve an originality pass too.

Ownership needs human work. Under the Gemini API terms Google claims no ownership of outputs, but may generate "the same or similar content for others," and paid-tier prompts and responses are not used to improve its products ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). The notes found no indemnity in those terms. The US Copyright Office holds that AI output is protected only where "a human author has determined sufficient expressive elements," and that prompts alone do not qualify ([copyright.gov](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html)). The Supreme Court declined *Thaler* on March 2, 2026 ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/the-final-word-supreme-court-refuses-to-hear-case-on-ai-authorship)). Protectable value therefore comes from human redrawing of faces and outlines, hand-composed fusions and final colour design, documented in layered source files and prompt logs. Every Gemini image carries a SynthID watermark ([Gemini image generation docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation)). Apple's current guidelines have no AI-asset rule beyond IP and copycat rules ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), and Play's AI policy targets in-app generation ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13985936?hl=en)). Plan honest disclosure on the store page rather than trying to strip the watermark.

## Conclusion

The three-layer split the brief asks for is not a compromise between readability and richness. It is what makes a 3D look affordable at all, because it confines rendering to the one surface that is static, framed, never background-keyed and never under a number. The deeper finding is that the game already contains its art direction. The 4096 apex structure dictates kits instead of 168 legendaries. The fusion colour table dictates each fusion's dominant colour. The projectile rule, "first element's shape in the second element's colour," is the fusion slot rule.
